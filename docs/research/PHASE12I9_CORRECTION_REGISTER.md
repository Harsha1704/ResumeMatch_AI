## 1. Discrepancy Inventory & Resolution Register

### Entry CR-01: Invalidation of the Cover-Hart Bayes Error Ceiling Claim
* **Historical Claim (Phase 12I.8):** "The Bayes error rate of the 3-class problem on this dataset is bounded below by $R^* \ge 28.0\%$, making 80%+ accuracy mathematically impossible." (Cited in `PHASE12I8_BENCHMARK_LIMITATIONS.md` and `PHASE12I8_HUMAN_FREE_RESEARCH_REPORT.md`).
* **Source of Error:** Mathematical category error. An arbitrary empirical finite-sample classifier error ($R \approx 0.44$) on $N=296$ validation pairs was substituted into the Cover-Hart (1967) large-sample inequality ($R^* \ge \frac{K-1}{K}[1 - \sqrt{1 - \frac{K}{K-1} R_{1-NN}}]$). The Cover-Hart bound holds strictly when $R_{1-NN}$ is the true asymptotic 1-Nearest Neighbor risk as $N \to \infty$ under continuous density assumptions.
* **Audit Verdict:** **`CLAIM INVALIDATED AND RETRACTED`**.
* **Certified Correction:** The Bayes error rate cannot be theoretically bounded from finite-sample classifier evaluations. Reaching 80%+ accuracy remains unachieved empirically due to label noise, 3-class ambiguity, and unobserved interview factors, but it is **not proven to be mathematically impossible**. The 80% benchmark remains an open research objective for cleaner, multi-modal data assets.

### Entry CR-02: Invalidation of Fisher Combined p-value for Correlated Multi-Seed Evaluations
* **Historical Claim (Phase 12I.8):** "Contrastive domain adaptation demonstrates a statistically significant +6.42 percentage point accuracy improvement over raw frozen sentence representations (Fisher combined $p = 7.15 \times 10^{-6}$)." (Recorded in `multi_seed_metrics.json`).
* **Source of Error:** Violation of the fundamental independence assumption of Fisher's combined probability test ($\sum -2 \ln p_i \sim \chi^2_{2k}$). All five seeds were evaluated on the **exact same 296 validation pairs**. Because model predictions share between $70.3\%$ and $80.7\%$ pairwise agreement, the five test statistics are strongly positively correlated. Applying Fisher's method under strong positive correlation yields a spuriously deflated, anti-conservative p-value.
* **Audit Verdict:** **`STATISTICAL METHODOLOGY CORRECTED`**.
* **Certified Correction:** Multi-seed evaluation demonstrates algorithmic stability against stochastic initialization across a single sample ($4$ of $5$ seeds achieve individual $p < 0.05$, with mean gain $+6.42\% \pm 2.71\%$). However, they do not constitute independent dataset replications. External generalizability requires independent evaluation on genuinely disjoint external datasets.

### Entry CR-03: Architecture Attribution of the 47.64% Dual-Unseen Result
* **Historical Claim (Phase 12G / 12I.8):** The 47.64% dual-unseen validation accuracy was labeled as "Cross-Encoder (DeBERTa-v3 / ModernBERT / MiniLM-L6 Cross-Encoder)" or "Cross-Encoder / Threshold-Calibrated".
* **Source of Error:** Attribution confusion. Forensic tracing of `scripts/phase12g/run_phase12g_research.py` (lines 171-235) and `experiments/phase12g/results/threshold_calibration_quality_audit.json` revealed that 47.64% was generated by applying a nested 5-fold candidate-cluster threshold rule ($\tau_{Good\_Fit} = 0.15$) to the probabilities of the **Contrastive InfoNCE Projector (Seed 42)**. No fine-tuned transformer cross-encoder PyTorch weights exist on disk.
* **Audit Verdict:** **`MISATTRIBUTED — OFFICIALLY RE-ATTRIBUTED`**.
* **Certified Correction:** The deep cross-encoder claim is marked **`UNVERIFIED`**. The 47.64% score is verified and fully reproducible, but its true architecture is the **nested out-of-fold threshold-calibrated contrastive projector (seed 42)**.

### Entry CR-04: Architecture Attribution of the 50.26% Official Held-Out Test Result
* **Historical Claim (Phase 12I.8):** In `model_comparison.json`, 50.26% held-out test accuracy was attributed to `threshold_calibrated_decision_rule`.

### Entry CR-05: Standardization of Latency Timing Boundaries
* **Historical Claim (Phase 12G / 12I.8):** Production Baseline Logistic Regression was reported with an inference latency of `0.0041 ms / pair`.
* **Source of Error:** Timing boundary mismatch. The 0.0041 ms ($4.1 \ \mu\text{s}$) measurement was taken strictly on `predict_proba(X)` executing linear matrix-vector algebra on pre-computed sparse feature matrices in RAM. It omitted text cleaning, structural parsing, regex/dictionary skill extraction, and TF-IDF vectorization. Comparing this classifier-only time with end-to-end neural cross-encoder latency (15.4 ms) was an invalid comparison.
* **Audit Verdict:** **`TIMING METHODOLOGY RECONCILED`**.
* **Certified Correction:** Under standardized benchmarking on real applicant pairs (`experiments/phase12i9/run_standardized_latency_benchmark.py`):
  - **Feature Extraction Latency:** $16.331 \ \text{ms}$ ($98.5\%$ of total).
  - **Classifier Predict Latency:** $0.242 \ \text{ms}$ ($1.5\%$ of total).
  - **End-to-End Latency:** Mean $16.573 \ \text{ms}$, Median $15.035 \ \text{ms}$, P95 $27.361 \ \text{ms}$.
